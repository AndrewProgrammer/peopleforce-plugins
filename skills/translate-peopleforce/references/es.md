# Spanish

Target variety: **Río de la Plata Spanish** (Argentina and Uruguay).

---

## 1. Register — voseo

*The negative-imperative rule below is documented by no public source, vendor generator or
competitor artefact — nothing outside this file recovers it, so keep §1 intact.*

- **Pronoun: *vos*.** If the slot is a formal legal notice, then *usted*.
- **Possessive stays *tu*.** `tu equipo`, `tu empresa` are already correct in
  voseo — leave them as they are.
- **Object pronoun stays *te*.** `te ayuda`, `te permite` are correct.
- **Second person plural: *ustedes*.** The pairing is **vos-singular / ustedes-plural**, and
  it matters for any line addressing a team. Measured across 10,035 Spanish turns: `ustedes`
  1030 rep / 245 prospect, singular `usted` effectively absent at 22/14, and **`vosotros`
  zero** — it is dead in this market, so a Peninsular plural is a tell rather than a variant.
  Note `usted` is the one address form prospects use *more* than reps (46 against 34), which
  is the general pattern: the customer is the more formal side of the call.

### Conjugation

Present tense: stress moves to the final syllable — *tenés*, *podés*, *sos*.
Imperatives: drop the *-r*, add the accent — **-ar → -á** (*Reservá*),
**-er → -é** (*Conocé*), **-ir → -í** (*Descubrí*). Irregular tuteo imperatives
regularise in voseo: *Elige* → *Elegí*, *Prueba* → *Probá*.

**Established CTA: `Reservá una demo`.** Use it as-is.

### Three slips to watch

These get through most often, because the voseo and tuteo forms look close:

| Slip | Correct |
|---|---|
| *puedes* | **podés** |
| *fíjate* | **fijate** (voseo drops the accent) |
| *considera* | **considerá** |

**Not a slip — negative commands.** A negative command takes the *tú* subjunctive:
`No manejes`, `No te preocupes`, `No pierdas`. The voseo-accented `No manejés` belongs
to other voseo regions, not Río de la Plata — so an unaccented form inside a negative
stays.

## 2. Explicitation

English, Polish and Ukrainian all strip words Spanish has to state, so a word-for-word
render comes out grammatical and foreign — the voseo can be perfect and the line still
read imported. Short copy is where it shows.

| The source drops | Spanish states it | Not |
|---|---|---|
| the article on a **mass or abstract** noun | `Gestioná el rendimiento` | `Gestioná rendimiento` |
| the preposition in a noun stack | `datos del empleado` | `datos empleado` |

**The article rule is about mass and abstract nouns, not about every noun.** A **plural
count** object keeps the bare plural, because the article would import definiteness the
English does not have: `No pierdas candidatos`, `Contratá recruiters`. §7's own rows
(`empresas de cualquier tamaño`, `tres de cada cuatro empleados`) depend on this. The
test: a quantity of countable things stays bare; a concept or an uncountable —
`el rendimiento`, `la rotación`, `el onboarding` — takes the article.

**The imperative's object is conditional.** If the object is not named earlier in the
same line or in the adjacent visual, attach the clitic: `Probalo gratis`. If the frame
already names it, the bare imperative is correct: `Probá gratis`. Decide which case you
are in and hold that choice across the whole set. The clitic takes **no accent** in
voseo: `probá` + `lo` → `probalo` (tuteo needs `pruébalo`).

**English `the same` splits two ways in Spanish, so the source cannot tell you which.**
Identity — one and the same thing — is `el mismo`. Similarity — another one like it — is
`igual`. Decide from what the sentence actually refers to:

| The source says | Write | Because |
|---|---|---|
| the same checklist (one document, reused) | `la misma checklist` | there is one checklist |
| the same first week (each new hire has their own) | `una primera semana igual` | `la misma` would claim one literal week everybody shares |
| the same result (matching, not shared) | `el mismo resultado` where it is one result, `un resultado igual` where two results match | the two readings are different claims |

Identity is the unmarked default and is right more often than not, so the error to watch is
`el mismo` applied where the source means *a similar one*. A judgement per sentence, and
**never a find-and-replace**. `mismo` agrees with its noun (`la misma`, `los mismos`).

**Agreement and pronoun marking are governed across a line break too** — the rule is
`SKILL.md` step 3; what is Spanish is that there is no case system, so only two things
reach across a boundary. A participle in a subhead agrees with the headline's noun
(`Una sola plataforma.` → `Diseñada`, not `Diseñado`). An answer takes the form its
question governs, including the `a`: `¿A quién le muestra PeopleForce tus datos
salariales?` is answered `Solo a mí`, not `Solo yo`.

**Gender.** If a line would describe the reader with a participle or an adjective
(`estás registrado`), rewrite it so it does not: `ya estás en la lista`, `tu registro
está confirmado`. Collective and abstract nouns, epicene nouns, second person instead of
third, and infinitives in UI labels all do this cleanly — product copy neutralises
through those devices, not through *desdoblamiento* (`los/las empleados/as`).

## 4. Anglicism policy

### Which terms translate

The glossary rows are the authority. The pattern to internalise: where Polish keeps
the English word, Spanish usually translates it — *panel* for dashboard, *selección*
for screening — and Spanish keeps acronyms, product, brand and customer names in
English per the do-not-translate lists. Where both languages translate (*nómina* /
*płace*, *cumplimiento* / *zgodność*, *flujo de trabajo* / *proces* for workflow),
take each from its own file: `pl.md` §4 states the Polish side.

Anglicised **verbs** go the other way and are normal in Argentine and Uruguayan
business speech — *setear*, *customizar*, *parametrizar* are fine in copy aimed
at this market.

### Four pairs that split by meaning

These are **distinct concepts, not competing translations.** Use each where it
belongs.

**recruitment**

- **reclutamiento** — attracting and sourcing candidates
- **selección** — choosing among them

Both can appear in one sentence, and often should.

**performance**

| Use | For |
|---|---|
| **Performance** | the product module name only |
| **gestión del rendimiento** | the management practice and the lifecycle stage — canonical per the lifecycle graphics |
| **evaluación del desempeño** | reviews and evaluations — corpus- and call-confirmed |

Collocation decides, not a concept split: *rendimiento* pairs with *gestión*
and with measured outputs; *desempeño* pairs with *evaluación*.

**onboarding**

- **onboarding** — the named process, the product capability, the thing you
  implement
- **incorporación** — flowing prose, the human experience of joining

**The gloss move**: on first mention, write the natural Spanish form and put the English in
brackets after it — *incorporación (onboarding)*. It buys both readings at the cost of two
words, and it works for any pair on this page where one form is the market's word and the
other is the one the reader may already know.

**feedback**

| Use | For |
|---|---|
| **feedback** | the default, and the named practice — *feedback continuo*, *feedback de 360*, *ciclos de feedback*, *dar feedback*, *feedback de desempeño* |
| **retroalimentación** | formal and legal register, where a borrowing would read out of place — *brindar retroalimentación*, *sesiones de retroalimentación* |
| **comentarios** | literal survey comments — a different concept, not a synonym |

Both are correct Spanish and neither is being retired; the split is **register, not sense**, so
`feedback` leads wherever ordinary marketing prose allows it. **The gloss move** applies with
the halves swapped, since here the loan is the natural form: *feedback (retroalimentación)*.

## 5. Punctuation and formatting

| Convention | Rule |
|---|---|
| Thousands | **`1.000`** — dot |
| Decimal | **`4,6`** — comma |
| Quotes | `“ ”` |
| `¿` `¡` | always open questions and exclamations |

**Percent is conditional.** Body copy: `30 %` with the space. A graphic, an ad or any
other fixed canvas: `30%` closed up — what the canonical lifecycle assets do, in every
instance.

**Dashes.** Spanish takes the **raya `—`** for bilateral incisos and for dialogue;
parentheses, a comma or a colon do the same work and are often better in short copy. If
a Polish or Ukrainian source carries the en dash `–`, render it as a raya, parentheses,
a comma or a colon — the en dash does none of those jobs in Spanish.

## 6. Structure

**Sentence case, question-led headings, and this section applying to articles only are all
set once, in `SKILL.md` step 1.** What follows is only what is Spanish — starting with the
Spanish-specific reason not to flatten a question to a noun phrase: it is how the content
gets found.

`¿Por qué…`, `¿Qué es…`, `¿Cuáles son…`

Common section headings: `Conclusión` to close, `Preguntas frecuentes` for an FAQ,
`Beneficios de…`, `Automatización de…`.

## 7. Calques

Source away, read each line as an HR specialist in Buenos Aires or Montevideo would —
someone who buys this software. Then check the calques below **against the rule, not by
ear**: they get past precisely because they are grammatical Spanish. They are where it
happens most often, not the whole set.

**`cada`.** Keep it wherever the source has a distributive: governed by a preposition
(`para cada miembro`, `en cada etapa`, `de cada cliente`) and when it opens a clause
(`Cada módulo se encarga de un proceso diferente`). Only one case takes a plural
totality instead — a verbless pair whose other half states a count, where the point is
one-against-many: `Una plataforma, todas las etapas`, not `cada etapa`.

Leave these to Spanish idiom, they are not distributives — except the ratio row, where
the Spanish idiom carries a `cada` of its own and a plural would be wrong:

| Construction | Spanish | Not |
|---|---|---|
| time span | `informes mensuales`, `16 horas por mes` | `cada mes` as a default |
| ratio | `tres de cada cuatro empleados` | a plural |
| any size | `empresas de cualquier tamaño` | `de cada tamaño` |
| reciprocal | `entre sí`, `ayudarse mutuamente` | `cada uno al otro` |

**Noun stacks and bare imperatives** are the other two silent calques — see §2.
