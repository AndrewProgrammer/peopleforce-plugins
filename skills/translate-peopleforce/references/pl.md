# Polish

## 1. Register — informal

Address the reader as **`Ty`** — capitalise `Ty`, `Twój`, `Ci` in direct address.
Second-person imperatives are standard.

**Polish also writes many lines in the first person plural** (`możemy`, `sprawdzamy`).
Reach for it where a second-person line would sound pushy: the we-form is ordinary Polish
marketing usage, not corporate hedging — and it carries no second-person form at all, so
nothing in it can slip register.

## 2. Explicitation

English and Spanish strip words Polish has to state, so a word-for-word render comes out
grammatical and foreign; short copy is where it shows. A Ukrainian source already marks
aspect and case — check them rather than rebuilding. **Possession does not carry across
from Ukrainian:** uk writes non-reflexive `ваш` by house rule, so rebuild `swój` from the
sentence's own logic.

**Gender.** Polish marks gender in the **finite past and predicate adjectives, and
nowhere else** — present, future, infinitive, imperative, dative experiencer and
possessive are gender-free with the reader still in the line. `Pan/Pani` is gendered too,
so there is no formal escape. The fix is to leave the finite past, **not** to remove the
reader. If a line would put the reader there, use one of these — **check the condition;
two of them change what the sentence says**:

- **A verbal noun** (`Twoje zatwierdzenia: trzy wnioski`) — always available, no trap.
- **A dative experiencer** (`udało Ci się skonfigurować`) — when the reader did something
  and you want to say so.
- **A present-tense state** (`Nie masz jeszcze uzupełnionego profilu`) — when reporting a
  state rather than an act.
- **An imperative** (`Uzupełnij profil`) — only when you actually want a command: it
  changes the speech act, so it is a decision for whoever owns the line.
- **Past tense on us** (`Zapisaliśmy Cię`) — **only when the product did the acting**. On
  a reader-as-actor line it reassigns agency and fails faithfulness as a mistranslation.

🚩 Masculine-personal plural agreement (`pracownicy zalogowali się`) has no neutral form.
Avoid the construction and say in the handover that you did — a native reviewer still has
to confirm what marketing register accepts here.

**Case and gender are governed across a line break** (`SKILL.md` step 3); what is Polish
is the forms: an answer takes the case its question governs (`Tylko mnie`, never
`Tylko ja`), and a subhead participle agrees with the headline's noun
(`Jedna platforma.` → `Stworzona`).

Three things an English or Spanish source will be missing: **the verb's aspect**, **the
reflexive possessive** (`Zarządzaj swoim zespołem`, never `Twoim`), and **the case links
inside a noun stack** (`zarządzanie cyklem życia pracownika` — a run of bare nominatives
is the calque).

**Aspect: meaning picks it, not the fact that it's a CTA.**

- **Perfective** for a single completed act — `Wypróbuj`, `Zarezerwuj`.
- **Imperfective** for the ongoing or habitual — `zatrudniaj, wdrażaj, rozwijaj` name
  continuing activities.
- **Imperfective after a negation** — `Nie zarządzaj`; a negated perfective is a warning
  about a mishap (`Nie zgub tego!`), almost never what marketing copy means.

**`swój` vs `Twój`:** English *your* gives no signal. `Twój` is correct when the reader
owns something the sentence is *about*; when they act on their own thing, it is `swój`.
**And before that choice: an English possessive often has no Polish counterpart at all.**
If the possessor is obvious, drop it (`zmienisz zdanie`, not `zmienisz swoje zdanie`);
keep it only where the owner would be genuinely ambiguous — only then does `swój`/`Twój`
apply. The test is whether Polish written from scratch would have the possessive, and a
dropped one is often the cleanest way to keep the reader out of a predicate adjective.

**`nieobecność` is the system's noun** — the record, the policy, the calendar — never a
word for a person's time off. The term map and the verb phrases (`wnioskować o czas
wolny`, never `wziąć nieobecność`) live on the glossary's *time off* and *leave* rows.

**English `the same` splits: identity is `ten sam`, similarity is `taki sam`.** Both
inflect with their noun; decide per sentence, never by find-and-replace. Identity is the
default, so the error to watch is `ten sam` where the source means *a similar one* — a
thing each person has their own copy of: `the same first week` is
`taki sam pierwszy tydzień`, since `ten sam` would claim one shared week.

## 4. Anglicism policy

**Which anglicisms are established is a glossary question, and the pl cell is the
answer** — including acronyms. Check the row per word; never generalise from a
neighbouring word or from another language's cell. Expect the cells to answer more
conservatively than Polish's reputation suggests: *workflow*, *payroll*, *compliance* and
*performance* all translate — no English survives in those cells — while *well-being*
splits, on the condition in its row's note.

## 5. Punctuation and formatting

Thousands take a **space** (`1 000`), decimals a **comma** (`4,6`). Percent is
**unspaced**: `30%`. Quotes are **`„ ”`**. Any dash that survives into Polish is the
**en dash `–`**, the myślnik — not counted, not budgeted, not matched to the source.

**The `X – to Y` definition takes the myślnik, and it is optional** — Polish keeps a
copula, so the dash before `to` is emphasis, not grammar. Neither adding nor removing it
is an error; hold one pattern through the piece.

### Punctuation around a quotation

**The sentence-final period goes after the closing quote** (`…nie ma zgadywania”.`); a
`?`, `!` or `…` belonging to the quoted sentence stays inside
(`„Ile to kosztuje?” — zapytała.`).

**An attribution before direct speech ends in a colon** (`Maria, dyrektorka HR: „…”`).
Where the attribution follows the quote, the myślnik with **no comma before it**:
`„…tydzień naprzód” – mówi Maria.`

**Sentence case, question-led headings, and this section applying to flowing text (and

## 7. Calques

Put the source out of sight and read each line as an HR specialist in Warsaw — someone
who buys this software — would read it. Then check the calques below by **applying each
rule to the whole text, not by ear**: these constructions are grammatical in Polish, so
the ear lets them pass. They are where it happens most often, not the whole set.

**`każdy`.** Keep it where the source has a distributive: governed by a preposition
(`na każdym etapie`) or opening a clause (`Każde państwo członkowskie UE definiuje własne
sankcje`). One case takes a plural instead — a verbless pair whose other half states a
count: `Jedna platforma, wszystkie etapy`, not `każdy etap`.

**Three constructions are Polish idiom, and `każdy` breaks all three.** A **ratio**:
`trzech na czterech pracowników`, never `z każdych czterech`. A **reciprocal**:
`ze sobą` / `pomagajcie sobie`, never `każdy drugiemu`. A **time span** is conditional: a
rate or recurrence takes the adverbial (`co miesiąc`, `miesięcznie`); counting one
specific month does take the distributive.

**One exception runs the other way:** *of any size* is `firmy każdej wielkości` — Polish
keeps `każdy` where Spanish and English drop it. 🚩 Awaiting native confirmation; do not
generalise from it.

**Aspect and `swój`** are the other two silent calques — see §2.
